## In one line
In a world where your first hand-hold shows you how the relationship ends, David sees himself holding Sarah's hand at
her deathbed after a long life and is overjoyed, until Sarah, terrified, tells him he was really pinning her arms so
she couldn't reach the emergency button.

## Why it works
1. **The rule is the title and the first sentence, and the picture proves it at once** (0.00-4.34): a demo couple
   (not the leads) hold hands at 1.25, a warm white flash fires on "you instantly see" (peak 2.25) and they are shown
   hugging, beaming (2.4-4.4). By 4.4 s the viewer has seen the rule work once. *heard + seen + measured*
2. **Dread is planted before the story starts** (4.42-7.88): three bad endings in 3.5 s (a can thrown in a fight, a
   cheater holding hands in front of a crying partner, a couple drifting apart). So when David reaches for Sarah's
   hand (11.6-13.2) the viewer fears the vision; the long-happy-life vision then lands as relief. *heard + seen*
3. **A false happy ending fills the middle** (13.40-22.24, 41-68% of the length): deathbed ("heartbreaking, yet
   beautiful"), then a sofa scene of a long life, then David opening his eyes "happy". The highest point sits
   immediately before the reversal "But Sarah wasn't smiling" (22.34). *heard + seen*
4. **The twist re-reads a scene the viewer already saw.** At 29.98 a hard cut (the only vision cut without a flash)
   returns to the same hospital set and nearly the same framing as 13.42, but old David's face is now a wide creepy
   grin and Sarah's a clenched-teeth grimace; the narration turns "gripping her hand" into "pinning my arms down". The
   face swap carries the twist. *seen + heard*
5. **The ending lands on the object named in the last words:** a whip-pan (31.08-31.32) from the bed to the red
   emergency button on the wall while the line ends "...the emergency button" (31.82-32.50). The video ends 0.19 s
   after the last word. *seen + measured*
6. **Every clause gets a picture event:** all 20 cuts land within 0.09 s of the first word of a clause, every flash
   peaks within 0.04 s of the first word of a clause, and the bass hits sit in the 0.16-0.26 s breaths between
   sentences (8.0, 13.25, 25.1, 29.95). Narration never stops (4.34 words/s). *measured*

Writing voice: the rule is second person present ("you hold hands", "you instantly see"); the story is third person
past ("David's first date with Sarah was perfect"); the twist is dialogue, first to second person ("You weren't
holding my hand to comfort me, David"). Sentences of 4-13 words, plain words, no slang and no jokes. Each sentence is
one shootable action. Feelings are named outright ("heartbreaking, yet beautiful", "happy", "confused", "in terror").
Adverbs carry the intensity and get the caps (INSTANTLY, NERVOUSLY, TIGHTLY, VIOLENTLY). "But" opens the reversal.

## 11. Principles to reuse / things not to copy
**Reuse:** state the rule as the first sentence and the title; prove it on anonymous extras before the story; show
the range of bad outcomes early so the viewer dreads the protagonist's turn; one clause per shot, cut on the clause's
first word; a 0.3 s warm flash in the edit as the sign that the rule fired, with the cut hidden at the white peak,
and a hard cut instead of a flash for the final shock; a false happy ending across the middle (41-68%), the
reversal opened with "But" right after the happiest beat, the twist at about 85% told in one fast line; re-stage an
earlier scene with new faces so the twist re-reads it; end on the object named in the last words and stop within
0.2 s; big 2-3-word captions centred at about 22% from the top, stressed words in caps, no emoji; face close-ups that
pull back to the pose; bass hits on structural turns.
**Don't copy:** the hand-holding premise and the murder twist (pinning her arms, the emergency button), the names
David and Sarah and their looks (striped polo; red cropped jacket with curly ponytail), the demo couple, the example
tableaux (the "BLOXY" can, the cheating trio), the deathbed and sofa scenes, this exact script.
