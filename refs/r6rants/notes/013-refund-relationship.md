## In one line
In a world where yelling "I want a refund" at someone pays back everything you spent on them but makes you forget
they existed, Jake gets rich refunding his ex and his fake friends, then refunds his mom when she grounds him, and
collapses: he has forgotten how to walk, talk and eat, because she taught him everything.

## Why it works
1. **A wish with a price, both inside 6 s** (0.00-6.02): free money for one sentence, "but the catch is, you forget
   they ever existed", and the demo girl fades out on screen as the catch is said (5.47-6.05). The catch is the plant
   for the twist. *heard + seen*
2. **Guilt-free abuse that escalates** (6.22-18.26): the targets are ones the viewer won't miss (a "toxic
   ex-girlfriend", "fake friends", for lunch money) and the payoff gets sillier (cash in both hands, then a golden
   toilet surrounded by money stacks at 17.0). The middle third is comedy. *heard + seen*
3. **The fatal use is played as suspense the viewer can predict** (18.44-25.02): grounded for a failed math test, Jake
   storms downstairs and yells it at his mom. The viewer knows the rule, so the 0.26 s hold on his grin (24.62-24.88),
   the only pause in the video (0.40 s) and the white flash (peak 24.98) are dread, not surprise. *heard + measured*
4. **The twist is the catch taken one step further** (27.06-31.58, from 85% of the length): forgetting his mom means
   forgetting everything she taught him, so he can't walk, talk or eat. It needs no new rule, and it turns a comedy
   into a moral in 4.5 s. *heard*
5. **The last image is the rule's own visual:** Mom fades out on "TAUGHT him EVERYTHING" (31.27-31.6) and the video
   holds the empty room for 0.3 s; it ends 0.32 s after the last word. *seen + measured*
6. **The camera points at whoever is named:** 7 swoops of 0.15-0.32 s carry the camera from the speaker to the person
   being talked about, each landing on the word that names them ("someone's", "forget", "toxic", "yelled", "fake
   friends", "mom", "her"). The cuts land on the first word of a clause: 16 of 19 within 0.07 s, the other 3 on a
   phrase start (15.82, "just to get back") or next to words the transcript timed poorly (28.33, 30.05). *measured + seen*

Structure: rule and catch, protagonist, escalation of use, "but" turn, fatal use, false win, twist from the catch.
Three sentences start with "But" and each opens a reversal (4.26 the catch, 18.44 the turn, 27.06 the twist). The
peak comes right before the turn ("incredibly rich", 17.52-18.26, then "But one evening" at 18.44). The twist line
is read fastest (5.53 words/s). Emotional arc: wonder, caution, glee (the middle is played for laughs), anger, dread,
false triumph, shock, grief. No tag after the twist; no sentence loop (ends "...taught him everything", starts "In
this world...").

Writing voice: the rule in second person present ("if you say", "you get back", "you forget"); the story in third
person past with short declaratives ("Jake became addicted." "Jake was furious."); the trigger phrase in quotes as
dialogue. Light slang and gamer words where they help the comedy ("overpowered", "toxic", "fake friends", "lunch
money"), none in the twist. Every sentence is one shootable action, and the caps land on the action or the stake
(INSTANTLY, ADDICTED, FURIOUS, STORMED, COLLAPSED, FORGOTTEN).
