## In one line
The same deadpan sunglasses narrator as 001 lists three things the teacher's pet does (her hand is up before the
question is finished, she jumps in when a classmate is slightly wrong, she reminds the teacher about homework), answers
each with a "bro" retort, then turns up behind her in the dark as a red-glowing demon.

## Why it works
1. **The 001 hook with a new type** (0.00-2.04): "Bro, why are teacher's pets so annoying?" over the same staging
   (narrator alone, "BRO", push-in, then pointing at the subject at 0.67). Every student knows the type, and the line
   takes a side. *heard + seen*
2. **Three behaviours, each answered by a retort**: hand up too early → "Relax lil bro. The question isn't going
   anywhere." (5.76); correcting a classmate → "Actually, it's..." cut off by "Bro! It's not that serious." (12.70);
   asking for the homework the class prayed was forgotten → "Bro, whose side are you on?" (21.78). A punch about every
   8 s, as in 001. *heard*
3. **Action and reaction in one camera move:** each retort is reached by a 0.05-0.16 s swoop from the pet to the
   narrator (5.72, 12.80, 22.00), not a cut, so the pet's action and his reaction read as one beat. Hard cuts are kept
   for new examples. *seen on strips*
4. **The viewer is given a side:** the examples escalate in who gets hurt (the teacher's question, one classmate, the
   whole class), and the last one makes the pet a traitor: the class sits in peace (14.82), the narrator prays (16.55),
   she asks "Didn't we have homework due today?" (20.55). *heard + seen*
5. **A tone flip for the ending:** the lights go out (23.52) and the narrator stands behind the pet as a red-glowing
   figure with a skull grin (24.20) on "I'm taking matters into my own hands". The narrator escalates instead of
   roasting. *seen*
6. **A comment prompt inside the set:** the whiteboard reads "answer in the comments!", "6+7=?" and "subscribe if you
   hate 67" with 🥀💀, on screen 2.53-4.50 and 7.17-8.70: a meme-number question that asks for comments without
   breaking the story. *seen* (its effect on comments: guess)

## 11. Principles to reuse / things not to copy
**Reuse:** the hook template (one word, "Bro"; narrator alone; push-in; the subject in a two-shot within 0.7 s); three
behaviours that widen in who they hurt, each closed by a short retort said to the subject; a short swoop to join an
action to its reaction, hard cuts for new beats; a meme-format line from the subject as a tag, interrupted by the
narrator; a narrator who switches between to-camera (accessory on) and in-scene (accessory off, eyes acting); a final
tone flip done with light (lights out, one coloured glow) that escalates the narrator instead of a roast; one
consistent sub-bass sound after each punch, with the next cut inside it; a comment prompt written on a prop in the set;
background extras that are jokes in themselves; character traits (the pet's glasses) drawn into every face texture of
that character.
