## 11. Principles to reuse / things not to copy
**Reuse:**
- The rant opening as a template: narrator alone, push-in plus arm snap, "Bro, why are [group] like this?" in about
  2 s, the subject beside him by about 1 s, a hit after the question, and the first example by 2.5 s.
- The body can be one escalating incident instead of a list, as long as a punch lands every 2-4 s.
- Question-and-one-word-verdict pairs, each verdict on its own caps caption.
- A flip at about 55% of the runtime ("when you finally get mad...").
- Faces as plot: fake crying exposed by a smirk swap on the word.
- Anger shown by tweening the head's colour before the angry face.
- Reveals by camera move that land on the payoff noun.
- A held still before a door flies open, for an entrance.
- Make the final idiom literal.
- The sibling look: the same hair, a smaller scale.
- A background comment-bait sign.
- Cut just before the stressed word.
- Use pauses on purpose: the calm before, room for a hit, the switch of speaker.

**Don't copy:**
- This script and its jokes: "boss fight", "my phone? gone", "you mad?", "calling for backup" with mom and a slipper,
  "throwing hands" as a window throw.
- The brother's "67" shirt with the grinning face and mango.
- The narrator's look and his "bro".
- The exact "COMMENT IF YOU SEE THIS" text.

So the formula is the first 2.5 s, the narrator in his own scenes, a face swap per line, the caption style, a beat with

- the body (a list or a story);
- cutting against moving the camera;
- the ending (a roast or a literal gag);
- how tight the loop is;
- the pace of speech and the use of pauses;
- how loud the bed is.
