## In one line
A boy explains his town's rule (at 13 you spend a night in the woods with a lantern, and something else comes back), tells
how his best friend's advice to blow the lantern out got him trapped in a tree while a copy of him went home, and begs his
little sister, who goes in tomorrow, not to make the same mistake.

## Why it works
1. **A full logline and a scare in the first 5 s.** The first sentence (0.00-5.20) gives the setting, the trigger, the
   ritual and the threat ("what comes back is never quite you"). On its last word the close-up face changes to a skull
   grin (4.92). Within 5 s the viewer knows the genre, the rule and the danger. *heard + seen*
2. **Each act answers one question and opens the next.** What is "swapped"? (23-26 s) Is Jack swapped? (26-36) Will the
   password protect Maya? (38-53) Did she really come back? (54-59) Whose rule is right? (59-72) What's in the dark?
   (79-104) Where did the real kids go? (113-127) Who lied? (127-133) Who's next? (148-157). A big question closes every
   10-25 s and a smaller beat lands about every 3.5 s (section 2). *heard*
3. **One planted word pays off three times, flipped each time.** "Thunderbird" is chosen at 42.44, saves Maya at 52.54
   (relief), comes out of the faceless double in Eli's voice at 103.62 (horror) and is said by fake Eli to fake Maya at
   143.18 (dramatic irony). *heard*
4. **A new picture for almost every phrase.** 153 shots, a hard cut every 1.03 s on average, 3-6 real cuts in every
   full 5 s window from start to end. 80% of cuts land within 0.15 s of the start of a spoken phrase. No shot lasts
   more than 2.45 s, and the voice never stops for more than 0.46 s. *measured*
5. **The light tracks the story.** Warm lantern light goes with trust and safety. Near-black blue goes with danger
   (mean luma 20/255, 82% of pixels below 32 after the lantern goes out). Pale foggy dawn goes with the "returned", and
   bright day goes with the town's denial. The grade switches on the word: "lantern" ends at 80.30 and the frame is blue
   at 80.37. *measured + seen*
6. **Scary but safe, with a heart.** The scares are drawn 2D faces and blocky heads stuck in bark. There's no gore and no
   death words, and the victims are alive. The ending is a plea to protect a little sister, and it loops into the opening
   (155.93 to 0.00). *seen + heard*

## 11. Principles to reuse / things not to copy
**Reuse:**
- **Opening:** a logline-as-rule in the first sentence (setting + trigger + ritual + threat), with a face-decal scare
  on its last word.
- **Story shape:**
  - a legend told in second person over a stand-in kid, then first person;
  - a rule the story tests, and a counter-rule from someone trusted;
