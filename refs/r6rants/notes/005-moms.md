behaviours. Two reveals pull against each other: the name is absurdly big, the request absurdly small. The ending is a
threat made literal, like 004's "taking matters into my own hands" but shown at once.

panic and the reaction ("what did I even do", "you summoned me"); present tense; sentences of 3-12 words; Gen-Z register
("bro", "nah bro", "lil bro", "just know it's over", "the craziest part"); hyperbole by simile ("like it's a crime
documentary", "like a fallen warrior"), each of which the picture takes literally.

## 11. Principles to reuse / things not to copy
**Reuse:** the hook template; a universal experience stated as a rule; one situation in three acts (rule, build to a
reveal, anticlimax); a beat before the payoff (a pause of about 1 s, with the caption arriving first); an escalating
list with one face per item and the light changing as it builds; every simile shown literally, with its own set or
effect; props with their own shots or moves (the threat prop, the broken thing, the anticlimax object); a deadpan
repeat of the anticlimax with the prop in hand; a last line made literal on screen; an ending on the opening location
so the loop restarts cleanly; one consistent sub-bass sound after punches, with the cut inside it; long takes for
builds and short shots for panic.
**Don't copy:** the script and its jokes ("Bartholomew Jamalathan Quadravious Thompson III", "reviewing my last 48
hours like it's a crime documentary", "summoned me like a fallen warrior", "to pass the remote", "I'm vanishing"); the
mom's look and this slipper gag as staged; the narrator's look; the catchphrase "bro"; the real-photo meme pictures of
real people (a rights problem as well), the anime poster and the window creature; this teal summoning effect.
