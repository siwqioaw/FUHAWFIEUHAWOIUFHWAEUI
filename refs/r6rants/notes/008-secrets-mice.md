## In one line
In a town where every secret said out loud becomes a mouse that runs to whoever it's about, the boy who keeps everyone's
secrets shouts his lost best friend's secrets into a blizzard to find him. The small mice freeze, so he shouts his own:
he's afraid Milo doesn't see him as a best friend. A horse-sized mouse carries him to Milo, and Milo answers with a
horse-sized mouse of his own.

## Why it works
1. **The rule is the first sentence, and every clause gets its own demonstration shot.** "In this town, every secret
   you say out loud turns into a gray mouse" (0.00-3.28). The mouse appears on "gray mouse" (2.6-2.75). Then come the
   feet (3.50), the run to the person (5.23), the whisper (7.03), tiny (9.12), big (10.28) and 300 (13.37). By 16 s the
   viewer knows the game well enough to predict it. *heard + seen*
2. **Every rule is a setup that the story pays off.**
   - "Runs straight to the person" (5.20) becomes the rescue plan (44.08).
   - "Small secrets make tiny mice" (8.28) becomes the three failures (54-62).
   - "Big secrets make big ones" (9.90) becomes the horse-sized mouse (72.04).
   - "Whispers it in their ear" (7.00) becomes the twist: too big to whisper, so the whole town hears (86.2-88.8).
   - "Every family keeps a cat" (19.18) becomes the closing joke: the cat faints (97.6).
   - Milo's three secrets (28.6-33.4) are the three shots fired (49-61). *heard*
3. **A try-fail ladder with a counter, then a pattern break.** Three shouted secrets, each failing a little further on:
   10, 20 and 30 steps (54.06, 57.98, 61.10). Each try uses the same shots: shout, puff, run, freeze. The first takes 8 s
   to set the pattern (48.7-56.6); tries 2 and 3 then come about 2.7 s apart (56.84, 59.48). The ice cubes pile up in
   frame. The fourth secret is the hero's own, which turns the comedy into a confession. *heard + seen*
4. **The mechanic forces the emotional beat, and the answer comes back through it without dialogue.**
   - Only a big secret makes a big mouse, so the hero has to say his own (67.40-70.86).
   - The confession is the longest stretch on one camera axis (66.02-71.17, 5.15 s, punched twice) and the loudest
     passage (-15.7 dBFS against about -18.8 for the narration around it).
   - Milo's reply is a second horse-sized mouse that runs to the hero (90.84-94.2): his secret is the same.
   *measured + heard*
5. **No dead air.**
   - 4.24 words/s, with no pause longer than 0.30 s.
   - A picture change every 1.1 s.
   - A new story fact about every 1.8 s (56 beats; my segmentation, listed in section 2).
   - The longest stretch without a new fact (72.04-75.90, 3.9 s) is filled with spectacle: a puff, the giant mouse and
     3 camera angles. *measured*
6. **The tone changes every 10-15 s, and it ends on a laugh.**
   - Comic rules (0-35).
   - Danger (35-48): a bass-heavy bed fades in at 37.75-39.0.
   - Comic failure (48-62), then tears (63-72).
   - Spectacle (72-80), then a comic twist (86-90) and warmth (90-96).
   - A closing joke: the cat faints and a tiny mouse walks past it (96-99.8). *seen + measured*

## 11. Principles to reuse / things not to copy
**Reuse:**
- Premise and rule:
  - "In this [place], [rule]" as the very first sentence, and the title.
  - Show each clause of the rule in its own shot within the first 15 s.
  - Make the rule a cheap reusable prop (a block model plus a white-sphere puff). Let its size and number carry the
    escalation.
- Story:
  - Write every rule as a setup and pay each one off, the throwaway ones last.
  - A best friend, a vow, then a crisis that forces the hero to break it.
  - Three tries with a visible counter, then a pattern break into the hero's own vulnerability.
  - Answer the emotional question through the mechanic, without dialogue.
  - A comic twist after the peak, and a closing joke that calls back an early throwaway line.
- Edit and sound:
  - A new picture about every 1.1 s, cut on clause starts, and punch in on the caption change when there's no new
    angle.
  - Hold one axis and push in slowly for the confession, and let it be the loudest moment.
  - Switch the music bed at the crisis.
  - Mouse's-eye ground shots and follow-cams for the creature.
  - Two-face mouth flaps for shouted lines.
  - Change the weather inside a shot (sky, then lawn to snow).
  - Cool fog for danger, return to saturated sun for the resolution.
- Captions: 1-6 words, high in the frame, white with a black outline, stressed words in caps, no emoji for a story.

**Don't copy:**
- This premise (secrets become mice) and its plot (a blizzard rescue by shouting secrets).
- Milo and his secrets (the duck nightlight, the shoes, the fence "stretch").
- The lines ("But a mouse that big can't whisper", "You idiot!", "It hasn't chased a mouse since").
- The hero's look (ash-brown hair, blue star jacket) and Milo's red hoodie.
- This mouse design (grey blocks with pink square ears).
- The fainting-cat closing joke.
