## In one line
A deadpan kid narrator in sunglasses lists three things the popular girl in his class does (late and laughing,
laughing through her presentation, fishing for compliments), each acted out in a school set, and ends on a roast.

## Why it works
1. **Instant, universal premise in the first 2 s** (0.00-2.06): "Bro, why are popular girls so annoying?" names a
   type every school kid knows, and takes a side. The title repeats it. *heard + seen*
2. **Rule of three with a punchline inside each example**: late + side-eye + laughing (2.2-9.3), presentation laugh +
   "Brad is asleep" (10.3-19.3), compliment-fishing + "Bro, we know" (19.5-27.6). Each punch lands about every 8 s.
   *heard*
3. **Faces do the acting.** Every line gets a new face on the character it's about: smug, side-eye, laughing, glaring,
   pouty, yelling. About 25 face changes in 28 s, each on the word it illustrates. *seen*
4. **The narrator is in the scene** as a character, so his reactions (annoyed close-ups at 9.6 and 26.4, the yell at
   17.4) work like reaction shots that cue the viewer how to feel. *seen*
5. **Picture gags reward rewatching:** a tumbleweed rolls through the silent class (22.5), the whiteboard is covered in
   brainrot memes (11-19), a "z" floats over sleeping Brad (18.5). *seen*
6. **It loops.** It ends on "Bro, we know." (26.3-27.6) and starts on "Bro, why...", same narrator, same energy, so
   the restart feels like a continuation. *heard + seen*

Structure: question, three escalating examples each with its own mini-punch, final roast. The turn in each example is
a contradiction (laughing at nothing; Brad is asleep; the silence before fishing).
Writing voice: first person, present tense, run-on "like she..." storytelling, sentences of 3-9 words, Gen-Z register
("bro", "side eye", "don't get me started"). The girl's quoted lines are short and in-character.

## 6. Animation and acting
- **Snappy pose-to-pose animation:** big poses reached in about 4-6 frames at 60 fps (0.07-0.1 s), then held with small
  drift. Example: 17.37-17.53, from head down on the desk to both hands on head, mouth open; then the arms swing down
  into a point by 17.8. *seen on frame strips*
- **Gestures carry the lines:** pointing at the subject or the camera, hands on head, arms spread, hands on hips,
  leaning back to laugh, a cute knees-bent pose.
- **Faces change on the word,** often mid-shot: no mouth movement or lip sync; the face texture swaps.
- **Effects in the scene:** blue square particles burst around the laughing girl (14.5-15.0); a tumbleweed prop rolls
  through (22.5-23.2); a floating "z" (18.5).
- Walks are short and simple (2.6-4.0). Can't tell from stills how smooth the in-betweens are: ask in the listener pass.

## 11. Principles to reuse / things not to copy
**Reuse:** a one-line premise as a question with a stance; three examples, each with its own punch; a narrator who is
also on screen; a face change on every line; snappy pose animation with held poses; wide to establish, close-ups for
reactions; set gags in the background; selective caps plus a trailing emoji; a bass hit in the pause after a punch;
an ending line that loops into the first.
**Don't copy:** this script and its jokes (Brad asleep, "I look so bad today / we know"), the narrator's look (brown
hair, purple round glasses, purple tee), the whiteboard text, the channel's catchphrase "bro".
