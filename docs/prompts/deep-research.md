# Deep research: Roblox story shorts

For any deep-research assistant. Paste the prompt below as it is. Save the report it writes as
`docs/research/roblox-shorts-landscape.md`, sources and all, or paste it back to the agent to file. It fills what the reference videos can't show: the channel's whole history, its peers,
the tools these animators use, and the platform's rules.

## The prompt

```text
I'm building an automated production pipeline for original vertical YouTube Shorts animated in Roblox: comedy skits
and short high-concept stories acted by blocky R6 Roblox avatars with expressive 2D faces, built and animated in
Roblox Studio, edited with captions, music and sound effects. My study set is the YouTube channel @r6rants
(https://www.youtube.com/@r6rants): 16 of its 51 Shorts passed 1 million views between March and November 2026. The
formats that hit: "Why do [group] do this" narrated rants (9.9M), "Mom VS Dad" music-only photo comparisons (9.6M),
emotional mini-stories ("He Ordered 2 Dinners Every Night", 6.4M), "If [someone] OWNED Roblox" (4.0M), and
high-concept "In This World, [rule]" stories (4.3M). I will make original work: I want to learn the craft and the
market, not copy this creator.

Research the following and write a structured report. Prefer sources from 2025-2026, cite a URL for every factual
claim, give numbers where they exist, mark each claim as verified (with source), reported (one source, unverified)
or inference (yours), and say plainly where you found nothing.

1. The channel @r6rants
   - Every Short you can find: title, publish date, length, views, and its format. Which formats hit, how often, and
     how that changed over time. Posting frequency. Long-form videos, if any.
   - When the channel took off, and what changed then (format, length, title style, look).
   - Anything the creator has said publicly about how they make the videos: tools, plugins, voices, music, team,
     schedule (video descriptions, community posts, interviews, Discord, other socials). Don't collect personal
     information beyond what bears on production.
   - What commenters say: what they quote, ask for, complain about.

2. The peer group
   - The 10-15 biggest channels making Roblox skit or story Shorts in 2025-2026 (R6 skits, "In this world" concepts,
     POV skits, "If X owned Roblox", school and family skits). For each: subscribers, typical and best views, formats,
     length, posting rate, look, voice approach, and the device they're known for.
   - What separates their million-view Shorts from their ordinary ones, where anyone has analysed it.

3. How these videos are made: the toolchain, step by step
   - Animation: Moon Animator 2 (keyframes, camera tracks, face changes, easing), Roblox's Animation Editor,
     Blender with R6 rigs, motion capture (Roblox Animation Capture from video, others). What do top R6 skit
     animators actually use, and how long does a 30-second Short take them?
   - Faces: how expressive 2D faces are done on R6 heads (face decal swaps, face packs, plugins), where the faces come
     from, and whether using them in monetized videos is allowed.
   - Avatars: how outfits, hair and accessories get onto rigs in Studio (catalog and UGC items through
     HumanoidDescription, free models), and the rules for using catalog items in videos.
   - Sets and light: where the detailed interiors and towns come from (Toolbox, paid kits, showcase builds, popular
     roleplay maps), and the lighting setups people use (Future lighting, post-processing presets).
   - Capture: recording Studio at 1080x1920 and 60 fps (OBS setups, viewport sizing, plugins, Roblox's own capture
     options), and any frame-by-frame rendering approaches.
   - Editing: which editors (CapCut, Premiere Pro, After Effects, DaVinci Resolve), caption styles (font names,
     colours, positions, word-by-word highlighting), zoom and shake presets, sound-effect packs.

4. Voices
   - What voices these channels use: their own voice, pitch-shifted voices, CapCut or TikTok text-to-speech,
     ElevenLabs or other AI voices, hired voice actors. Name specific stock voices if they're widely recognised.
   - Costs and licences for commercial use of the main TTS and voice-cloning tools as of 2026.
   - YouTube's current rules on disclosing altered or synthetic content, and whether AI voices on animated Roblox
     videos fall under them.

5. How YouTube Shorts are ranked and judged in 2025-2026
   - The metrics that matter now (engaged views, viewed versus swiped away, average percentage viewed, loops,
     shares, comments) and how a new Short is tested and spread. Use YouTube's own statements first (Creator Insider,
     YouTube Help, Liaison posts), then creators who have shared real analytics.
   - Length: what works for comedy skits versus stories, and how the 3-minute limit changed things.
   - Hooks, on-screen text, titles, hashtags, first-frame thumbnails, posting time and frequency: what has evidence
     behind it and what is folklore.

6. Writing that holds a scroller
   - Proven structures for 10-40 s comedy skits and 30-160 s high-concept stories: hooks, the rule of three,
     escalation, open loops, twists, endings that loop back to the start. Cite screenwriters, editors or studies.
   - Why "In this world, [rule]" premises work, and how the best ones are built.

7. Policy and legal risk for a channel like this
   - YouTube's monetisation policy on "inauthentic" (formerly "repetitious") content, updated July 2025, and how it
     treats mass-produced or AI-assisted videos. What has happened to channels that automated production.
   - "Made for kids" and COPPA for Roblox animation: who decides, what it changes (comments, ads, reach), and how
     similar channels classify themselves.
   - Roblox's guidelines for creators using Roblox's name, logo and look in videos and thumbnails.
   - Music and sound effects in Shorts: YouTube's audio library and Shorts sound library, Content ID on popular
     songs and meme sounds, and safe sources for both.

8. The audience
   - Who watches Roblox skit Shorts (age, region, device), what they find funny, and the comment culture (for
     example the 😭🙏 title emoji).

Finish with:
- a table of the 20 most actionable findings for a new channel in this niche, each with its source;
- a list of open questions the evidence doesn't settle.
```
