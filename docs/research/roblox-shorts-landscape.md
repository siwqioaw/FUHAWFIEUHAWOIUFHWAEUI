Readings [I]:
- **Hit rate tracks how universal the subject is.** Rants about moms, popular girls, teacher's pets and little brothers hit 50% of the time. Generic romance and drama skits never reached 1M.
- **Emotional stories are a high-variance format.** One reached 6.4M; the other three stayed under 321K.
- **"In This World" hits came early in the run.** The first five went 688K → 4.3M → 359K → 2.1M → 1.3M. The last three, all posted within three days in late October, reached 83K–440K. That fits both premise fatigue and weaker premises. The weak ones ("a photo of your wife appears in your pocket") lack the immediate visual rule of the strong ones ("can't see colours until you touch your soulmate").

Takeaways:
- **Posting rate.** 48 Shorts between 9 Feb and 4 Nov 2026 is about 1.2 per week. Months ranged from 1 post (May, June) to 10 (August, October) [V].
- **Rants were dropped despite hitting.** The channel stopped "Why do X" rants after April even though half of them passed 1M. It then switched format roughly every 4–6 weeks. A likely cause is the hiring of freelance animators who are given scripts (see 1.5) [I]. Each switch copied a format already proven elsewhere in the niche (see section 2) [I].
- **Length is creeping up.** It went from about 25 s in the spring, to about 11 s in late summer, to 30–35 s in the autumn and 77–157 s in November. "The Lantern" at 157 s reached 2.1M in two days, so long stories are being distributed [V for the numbers, I for the reading].
- **Title style.** Spring titles used ALL-CAPS keywords with the 😭🙏 / 💀🙏 emoji pair. Story titles dropped the emoji ("He Ordered 2 Dinners Every Night"). The "In This World" titles spell out the whole rule [V].

### 1.6 What commenters say

From the top 20 comments on six Shorts [V]; the patterns are [I].

- **They quote punchlines.** "BRO, WE KNOW" and "BRAD IS ASLEEP" are repeated across dozens of comments, with up to 2.1K likes each, on "Why Do POPULAR Girls Do This". A short, reusable catchphrase is what spreads.
- **They hunt for background Easter eggs.** Commenters point out a "67 kid", c00lkidd, and Guest 1337 in "Mom VS Dad". On "He Ordered 2 Dinners" they spot "the person in the window" (286 and 233 likes) and "pictures on the building changing". The creator's only community post leans into this ("how many times did the 67 kid appear?"). Hidden details drive rewatches and comments.
- **They rewrite the joke.** The top comments are often new versions of it: "Moms old photos: Normal / Dads old photos: WW2 🗿" (350 likes), and the long "SOCOM unit [REDACTED]" joke (505 likes).
- **They ask for "Part 2" and send story ideas.** Both happen most on the "In This World/Town" stories ("Plsssss part 2", full premises pasted as comments).
- **They argue about the premise.** On the soulmate Short: "he never touched his bestfriend till that day?" (824 likes), and "soulmate doesn't mean partner" (3.8K). Premise loopholes create comment threads.
- **Complaints:**
  - A recurring "GET OUT" tag gag at the end of videos ("“GET OUT” at the end ruins it all"; "I'm glad there's no 'GET OUT' in the end", 302 likes).
  - Comparison to obby-gameplay rant channels ("Finally a rant without a obby", 197 likes), which shows animated rants stand out against gameplay-footage rants.
- **Age signals.** "Bro I'm 16 and I cried", plus heavy use of 😭🙏 💀 🗿 🥀. This is a teen-skewed audience.

## 3. How these videos are made: the toolchain

### 3.1 Animation
- **Moon Animator 2.**
  - Status [V]: by xsixx, sold in USD at $19.99 ("33% off", so about $29.99 normally), 93% positive, updated 30 Oct 2026 ([Toolbox API](https://apis.roblox.com/toolbox-service/v1/items/details?assetIds=4725618216), [price page](https://thetoolstrunk.com/how-much-is-moon-animator/)).
  - Features [R] ([DevForum guide](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/getting-started-with-moon-animator-2-unofficial/476330)):
    - Per-keyframe easing; the guide recommends slow-in/slow-out over linear.
    - Group, Mirror, Looper, Time Stretch and Wiggler tools.
    - A camera CFrame track with FOV (20–50 suggested) and AttachToPart.
    - A subtitle track.
    - Property animations that don't export to Roblox, which doesn't matter if you screen-record playback [I].
  - "Free Moon Animator" sites are leaks or spam [I].
- **Roblox Animation Capture.** Body capture from video works on R15 only (≤15 s clips, one person, stable camera). Face capture needs Dynamic Heads. Neither drives classic R6 decal faces [V] ([Roblox docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/animation/capture)). For R6 skits it is useful only as R15 reference [I].
- **Blender.** The free "R6 IK + FK" rig (2025) and Cautioned's Blender Animations plugin round-trip animation through .rbxanim. Version 2.0+ adds Live Sync [V/R] ([rig thread](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-ik-fk-blender-rig-v222/3586405)). This route suits teams that already work in Blender, or that want rendered output instead of a viewport recording [I].
- **What the top channels use.** Moon Animator is the only tool credited publicly: by r6rants through a hashtag, and by Mashles in a description [V/R]. Nothing else was found.
- **Time per Short.** I found no credible figure. The best proxy: r6rants asks applicants for their turnaround on a 35–50 s piece, and its 15–20 s test is treated as a meaningful job [V]. A Fiverr seller prices 20–30 s as a basic tier [R] ([listing](https://fiverr.com/pedromorton/create-roblox-animation-for-youtube-shorts)).

[V] ([DevForum announcement](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/completing-the-dynamic-head-migration/4301387)). A community "Dynamic2Classic" converter exists [R] ([thread](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/dynamic2classic-convert-back-dynamic-faces-to-classic/4540247)).

Some effects only show at the top Editor Quality [V] ([docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/post-processing-effects)). Lighting technology options include Future; LightingStyle, ShadowSoftness and ExposureCompensation are adjustable [V] ([Technology enum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/Technology), [lighting](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/lighting)).

### 3.5 Capture
- **Roblox staff capture guide (Feb 2024)** [V] ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/tips-for-capturing-high-quality-screenshots-and-videos-in-studio/2723897)):

## 5. How Shorts are ranked and judged (2025–2026)

**Metrics**
- **Two view counts.** Since 31 Mar 2025, a Short's public view counts every start and replay, with no minimum watch time [V] ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)). "Engaged views" (watched past an unpublished number of seconds) drive YPP eligibility and Creator Pool revenue. YouTube's 19 Aug 2026 explainer says recommendations are unchanged and still run mainly on engaged views [V] ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)).
- **Qualified views.** Since August 2026, eligibility uses "qualified views": engaged views on public Shorts only, excluding ad views and loops [V] ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-monetization-qualified-watch-hours-shorts-views/)). That YPP thresholds double for new applicants from 1 Feb 2027 is [R] (one source, unverified).
- **Viewed vs swiped away** replaces impressions and CTR for Shorts (Rene Ritchie) [V] ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/renes-top-five-march-4-2024/)). YouTube publishes **no threshold**. Benchmarks such as "70%/80% viewed" are folklore and contradict each other [V] ([SEJ](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953)).
- **Loops, shares, comments, average percentage viewed.** No YouTube statement on their weight. A Buffer claim that viewed-vs-swiped drives distribution was refuted 1–2 in verification.

**Testing and spread**
- **Seed audience.** A new Short goes to a "seed audience" and widens or tapers from there; "the audience is the algorithm" (Todd Sherman, Creator Insider 2023) [V] ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU)). The "explore/exploit phases" and "stuck at 2,000 views" ideas are vidIQ and tool-site framing [R].
- **Originality.** On 1 Oct 2026 YouTube said it will "further prioritize original content" in Shorts and reduce the reach of re-uploads [R] ([YouTube Help community](https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423/prioritizing-original-content-on-shorts?hl=en)).

**Length**
- **The 3-minute limit.** Shorts can be up to 3 minutes since 15 Oct 2024 [V] ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877)). Sherman: there is no ideal length, "to what length do you need to tell your story?" [V] ([Outlierkit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)).
- **Data.** Paddy Galloway's 2023 study of 3.3B views found 50–60 s Shorts averaged the most views. It predates the 3-minute limit and is correlational [V] ([thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)). No rigorous 60–180 s data was found.
- **What the niche shows** [I]:
  - Comedy gags work at 7–20 s (Clutchmaker, Roblonarium, r6rants's "Mom VS Dad" at 11 s and 9.6M).
  - Rants work at 20–32 s.
  - Rule-stories work at 30–180 s. The Gneef and Danty channels live at 120–180 s, and r6rants's 157 s "Lantern" reached 2.1M in two days.

## 6. Writing that holds a scroller

**Comedy skits, 10–40 s**
- **Game of the scene (UCB).** Find the "first unusual thing", then heighten it by asking "if this is true, what else is true?" [V] ([Wikipedia, citing the UCB Manual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_of_the_scene); [Will Hines](https://willhines.substack.com/p/teaching-game-2-heighten-and-explore)). In a short skit, show the unusual thing in frame 1, play 2–3 heightening beats, then cap it with a turn [I].
- **Rule of three.** Two beats set a pattern and the third breaks it [V, craft consensus] ([Ken Levine](https://kenlevine.blogspot.com/2017/07/the-comedy-rule-of-3s.html?m=1)). The r6rants "Why do X" rants follow this structure: list behaviours, then a final escalating one [I].
- **"But/therefore", never "and then"** (Trey Parker and Matt Stone at NYU, 2011) [V] ([TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/south-park-creators-visit-nyu-class-lessons-storytelling-30806)). Hoyos's "but/so" method is the Shorts version [R].
- **A quotable line.** The r6rants comments show that the shared unit is a two-to-four-word punchline ("BRO, WE KNOW") [V data, I conclusion]. Write one deliberately.

**Stories, 30–160 s**
- **Open loops.** Loewenstein's information-gap theory (1994): curiosity is a feeling of deprivation over a knowledge gap the viewer can perceive. It needs some prior knowledge, so the gap must be sized right [V] ([summary](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/)).
- **The Zeigarnik caution.** A 2025 meta-analysis found no memory advantage for unfinished tasks, only a tendency to resume them [V] ([Ghibellini & Meier 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)). Use "people want to finish", not "people remember the unfinished".
- **Twists and loops.** Hoyos uses foreshadowed payoffs and endings that replay into the start [R] ([vidIQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/how-jenny-hoyos-actually-makes-viral-shorts)). Loops raise public views, not engaged views [V, section 5].
- **Gap.** No academic study of retention in 30–160 s stories was found.

**Why "In this world, [rule]" premises work**
- **The theory.**
  - Blake Snyder's high concept: you instantly know "what is it", and the logline promises "more" [V] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat!:_The_Last_Book_on_Screenwriting_You%27ll_Ever_Need)).
  - The premise works as a stated first unusual thing plus a well-sized curiosity gap. The viewer knows the rule but not its consequences [I].
- **How the strong ones are built** [I, from the r6rants and peer view data]:
  1. Change one rule only.
  2. Make the rule visible on screen within 1–2 s: colours, a counter overhead, mice.
  3. Build in irony, where the rule's benefit becomes its cost (seeing the end of a relationship).
  4. Escalate with "if this, then what else?"
  5. Pay off with a consequence the viewer could have predicted but didn't: the best friend is the soulmate.
  6. Leave one loophole for commenters to argue over.
- **Weak premises** need explaining, have no visual tell, or have no irony ("a photo of your wife appears in your pocket", 83K).

## 7. Policy and legal risk

### 7.1 Inauthentic content (YPP)
- **The July 2025 change.** On 15 Jul 2025, "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content". YouTube called it a minor update to rules that already applied [V] ([YPP policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392), [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755)).
- **What the policy rules out** [V, same page]:
  - Videos that are "mass-produced, generic, repetitive", including "templated storylines".
  - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates".
  - **"Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome."**
  - When automated tools are used, the final product "must still demonstrate your creative vision".
- **AI is not banned.** Rene Ritchie: AI channels stay eligible if their uploads are not low-value [V] ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/youtube-clarifies-monetisation-policy-targeting-inauthentic-content-9493155)).
- **Enforcement** [V]:
  - January 2026: 16 channels with about 35M subscribers and 4.7B views were terminated or wiped ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage), [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2589917/youtube-deletes-47-billion-views-worth-of-ai-spam-videos-in-major-platform-crackdown)).
  - Kapwing found 278 "AI slop" channels among about 15K top channels, with 63B views, many of them looping kids' animation. 21% of the first 500 Shorts served to a fresh account were slop ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/2068701/youtube-video-ai-slop-study/)).
  - Faceless creators who don't use generative AI report collateral demonetisation [R] (TNW).
- **What this means for an automated pipeline** [I]. Automating the mechanics (rigging, capture, captions, rendering) is fine. The risk sits in writing, structure and outcomes that never vary. Keep variety in premise, cast, set and resolution. Keep script drafts, project files and edit history as evidence for appeals.

### 7.2 Made for kids and COPPA
- **Who decides.** The creator, who carries the legal responsibility. YouTube's systems can override the setting, and the creator gets one appeal per video [V] ([YouTube Help 9528076](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076), [9527654](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654)).
- **The FTC factors** include subject matter, **animated characters**, child-oriented activities, language, and the audience's actual age [V] ([FTC 2019](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/09/google-youtube-will-pay-record-170-million-alleged-violations-childrens-privacy-law); $170M settlement).
- **What MFK switches off** [V] ([9527654](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654)):
  - Comments, the notification bell, personalised ads, Miniplayer, cards and end screens, save to playlist, and memberships.
  - Posts, if the whole channel is set to MFK.
- **Reduced reach** is reported only by creators [R]. Comments are the main engagement and discovery signal in this niche, so this matters (section 1.6) [I].
- **COPPA amendments.** In force from 23 Jun 2025, with full compliance due by 22 Apr 2026 [V] ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches)).
- **How peers classify themselves.** No data. @r6rants has comments on, so it is not MFK [I]. Sonic fan animators were auto-flagged MFK and lost appeals [V] ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/gaming-news/sonic-content-on-youtube-is-being-flagged-as-made-for-kids-destroying-creator-livelihoods-3223238)).
- **Implication** [I]. Bright blocky animation hits several FTC factors. Writing about teen life (school, crushes, siblings, jobs) and keeping kid-coded words out of the metadata supports a "not made for kids" setting. It won't guarantee it.

### 7.3 Roblox's name, logo and look
- **Current Help page**, read in a browser on 6 Nov 2026 [V] ([Name and Logo Guidelines](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001708126-Roblox-Name-and-Logo-Guidelines)):
  - The Roblox logo and Tilt are **not permitted** for general use. Only the official "Now on Roblox" badge is, and that is for promoting a game.
  - Don't imply partnership or endorsement.
  - "Roblox" and "Blox" are barred from game titles and on-platform creator names.
  - The Roblox name and "Classic Roblox Avatars (R6 and R15)" may not be used in "commercial content", such as film, TV, books, NFTs and merchandise, without a licence.
- **The 2022 DevForum guidance** from Roblox Developer Relations is more permissive for creators [V] ([post](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/update-to-roblox-licensing-program-brand-guidelines/1843674)):
  - Videos with R6/R15 avatars "are not restricted", and creators may keep monetising them "through YouTube ad revenue".
  - Channels with "Roblox" or "Blox" in the name should say they are not affiliated.
  - TV or film needs approval.
- **Reading the two together** [I]. Ad-funded YouTube Shorts with R6 avatars are tolerated, and the whole niche depends on that. Merch, sponsorships built on the avatars, licensing a series, or anything that looks like "TV/film" moves into licence territory.
- **Practical rules** [I]:
  - Keep the logo out of thumbnails and covers.
  - Use "Roblox" only descriptively in titles and hashtags.
  - Keep "Roblox" and "Blox" out of the channel name, or add a "not affiliated" line.
  - Don't put R6 characters on merchandise.

### 7.4 Music and sound effects
- **The 60-second line.** **Any Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim is blocked worldwide.** Shorts Audio Library songs can be used for up to 90 s in a 3-minute Short [V] ([YouTube Help 15424877](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877)). This bites hard on 77–157 s stories [I].
- **Revenue share.** One licensed track halves the Short's contribution to the Creator Pool, and two leave one third [V] ([Shorts monetisation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)). So music-only formats like "Mom VS Dad" earn less per view when they use licensed songs [I].
- **Safe sources:**
  - YouTube Audio Library: no Content ID claims; credit Creative Commons tracks [V] ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882)).
  - Freesound: CC0 free; CC BY needs credit; **CC BY-NC cannot be used in monetised videos** [V] ([FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)).
  - Epidemic Sound: your channel is safelisted while subscribed, and videos published then stay covered [V] ([Epidemic](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/how-we-handle-youtube-content-id-claims/)).
  - Pixabay: some tracks are registered with Content ID, so keep the licence certificate [R].
  - Uppbeat's free tier: 3 downloads a month [R].
- **Popular songs and meme sounds.** No specific rule was found. Assume any commercial song or ripped meme clip can be claimed. Use them only in Shorts under 60 s, or recreate the sound yourself [I].
