# Concepts: from a topic to the one premise worth making

The owner may give a premise ("In this world, your pet narrates your life") or only a topic ("siblings", "school
lunch"). Either way: generate wide, test hard, pick one, and write down why.

## What the channel's numbers say

Across all 51 of @r6rants' Shorts (`docs/research/roblox-shorts-landscape.md`), the hit rate follows how universal
the subject is:

| Format | 1M+ hits | What made the hits |
|---|---|---|
| "If [type] OWNED Roblox" | 2 of 2 | a type everyone knows (a mom, a gold digger) let loose on rules everyone knows |
| "Why do [group] do this" rants | 4 of 8 | a group every viewer has met (popular girls, moms, little brothers, teacher's pets) |
| "In this world, [rule]" | 4 of 9 | a rule you get in 2 s whose benefit becomes its cost |
| emotional stories | 1 of 4 | a heart under the hook |
| relationship drama | 0 of 5 | (avoid: narrow, and adult) |

So: universal subjects first, a rule or a type the viewer can already picture, a visual that explains itself.

## Generate: ten candidates, never one

Write ten, across at least three formats, before choosing. Mines that work for a teen audience:

- **School:** group projects, substitute teachers, the cafeteria, gym class, the last five minutes of class, tests,
  homework excuses, lockers, field trips, the new kid, the teacher's pet, the class clown, presentations, detention,
  the school bus, picture day, fire drills, the bell.
- **Home:** moms, dads, little brothers, older sisters, grandparents, chores, curfew, the family group chat, dinner,
  the Wi-Fi password, "who ate my food", road trips, the TV remote.
- **Friends and phones:** the group chat, "seen", the friend who's always late, sleepovers, battery at 1%,
  notifications, the friend who copies you, birthday parties.
- **Gaming (Roblox culture, never Roblox's brand):** lag, the noob, the rich player, the try-hard, the friend who
  quits when losing, the admin, rage-quitting, the AFK teammate.
- **Moments:** first day vs last day, Monday vs Friday, 2 PM vs 2 AM, summer vs school, expectations vs reality.

**Rule generators** for "In this world" stories (each makes a premise in one sentence):

1. *Every time you [common act], [visible consequence].* (Every time someone talks about you, you hear a ding.)
2. *You can see [hidden thing] over everyone's head.* (How many days until they move away.)
3. *[An abstract thing] becomes [a physical thing].* (Secrets turn into mice; lies into pebbles in your shoe.)
4. *When you turn [age], you get [one thing], forever.* (One question the truth must answer.)
5. *You can [power], but [price paid somewhere else].* (Skip any day; someone else lives it twice.)
6. *[Everyone] must [rule], except [one exception].* (Everyone tells the truth on Fridays.)
7. *[Object] shows [state].* (Your shoelaces untie when someone's thinking of you.)

**Type generators** for rants and "owned": *Why do [group] always [habit]?* and *If [type] owned Roblox* (the
type's habits become server rules, with in-world notices).

## Test: the three premise tests, then the score

Kill a candidate that fails a premise test:

1. **Shown in 2 s.** Can the rule or the type be shown on screen, without words, in two seconds? (A ding and a bell
   over a head: yes. "A photo of your wife appears in your pocket": no, it flopped at 83K.)
2. **Benefit becomes cost.** Does the rule's upside turn into its downside (or the type's habit backfire)? That's the
   story's engine and the twist's fuel.
3. **One loophole.** Is there one thing commenters will argue about ("so does it ding for nice things too?")? That's
   the comment section.

Then score the survivors, 0-3 each, and take the highest (ties: the more universal):

| | 0 | 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Universal | a niche group | everyone has one (a mom, a group project) |
| Visual | needs explaining | the picture explains it |
| Quotable | no line to repeat | a 2-4 word line people will type ("WE?!", "Seen.") |
| Escalation | one joke | three beats that each top the last |
| Twist / punch | none | the rule bites back, or the last example is absurd |
| Loop | ends flat | the last shot leads into the first |
| Original | close to a reference | nothing like the 16 references or the variety ledger |
| Safe | needs care | teen-safe as is |

Record all ten with their scores in `videos/<slug>/notes.md`.

## Originality

- Read the pick against every reference transcript (`refs/r6rants/transcripts/`): no shared jokes, premises, lines or
  characters. A shared *format* is fine; a shared *premise* isn't.
- Read it against the variety ledger (`videos/LEDGER.md`): never the same cast in the same situation with the same
  outcome twice (YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule names exactly that).
- Add the pick to the ledger when the video is delivered.

## From pick to logline

One sentence each:

- **Logline:** who, what rule or habit, what goes wrong, how it turns.
- **The quotable line.**
- **The hidden detail** (a whiteboard joke, a sign, an extra doing something odd in the back).
- **The loop:** how the last frame leads into the first.

Then go to the format's playbook (`formats/`) and fill its beat table.
