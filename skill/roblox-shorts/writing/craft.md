# Craft: jokes, twists, the teen voice

## Comedy (family A)

- **Open on the premise, said in the first second,** by the narrator on screen. Our narrator opens with "Nah," or goes
  straight into the question; never the references' "Bro," opener.
- **Three examples, each topping the last,** each closed by a retort or a punch. A punch every 2-8 s. The third is the
  absurd one.
- **The punch is short:** 1-4 words, often in the deep voice ("Seen.", "Denied."), or a one-word outburst ("WE?!").
  A sub-bass boom lands right after it, and the next cut lands inside the boom.
- **Show every figure of speech literally:** "he's basically a ghost" (a kid walks through him), "dead silent" (a
  tumbleweed), "my brain left" (a top-down of him blank).
- **Pauses are beats:** split a line where the joke needs air and leave 0.2-0.5 s in the shot timing; the voice
  processing cuts pauses inside a line.
- **End by escalating the narrator** (his face, his fate) and cut hard so the replay loops.
- **Faces change on the word**, mid-shot: smug on the setup, side-eye on the retort, angry on the stressed word, dead
  at the end. A face change is the cheapest, strongest gag.

## Stories (family B)

- **The rule is the first sentence** and the title. Demonstrate it on strangers by 4-6 s.
- **A false high, then "But"/"Then"/"Until".** The twist comes from the rule itself, one step deeper, at about 85%,
  in one fast sentence; the last image is the rule's own visual on the last word; stop within 0.3 s.
- **Continuous narration**, 4.2-4.7 words a second, no silence; characters' lines in the storyteller's voice, told
  apart by delivery tags (`[softly]`, `[whispering]`).
- **One face per shot**, chosen for its sentence; cut on the first word of each clause.

## Music formats (family C)

- A header from frame 0, three shots a side (or four beats per type), the second side outdoing the first, cuts on the
  beat, a reaction or "Me:" to close.

## The teen voice

- Write how a 14-17 year old talks today, lightly: "nah", "lowkey", "be so for real", "it's giving", "no shot",
  "caught in 4K". One or two per video, not every line.
- Never kid-coded: no "kiddos", "silly billies", "boo-boo", nothing a toddler show would say.
- Roast a type, never a person. Insults stay soft ("goofy", "down bad"); no slurs, no bodies, no appearance roasting of
  real groups.

## Lines that land in TTS

- One idea per line; 4-14 words; the stressed word in CAPS (`that ONE person`).
- Tags before the words they colour: `[excited]`, `[sarcastic]`, `[deadpan]`, `[shouting]`, `[whispers]`, `[sad]`,
  `[urgent]`, `[softly]`, `[warmly]`, `[deep voice]`. A tag holds until the next.
- Numbers as words. Homophones are fine (the check accepts "seen"/"scene").
- Generate 2 takes of any line that carries a joke; `voice.py` keeps the clearest, liveliest one that passes.
